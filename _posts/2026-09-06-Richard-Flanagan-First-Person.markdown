'Absorbing' is the word that comes to my mind when I think about this novel. At times a critique of publishing industry and at others an inner reflection on the process of writing itself (or the difficulty of it), This novel draws you into the world of Kif, Ray, Ziggy, and Sully from the get go and never loosen the grip till the last page.  Kif Kehlmann (Kif) is a struggling author from Tasmania, who is hired to ghost write a memoir of a notorious corporate criminal Sigfried Heidl (Ziggy). With his desperation for a stable financial and career prospects, Kif is forced to share an office with Ziggy, and spend a significant time trying to extract any meaningful, coherent picture of Ziggy's life which proves to be anything but easy. We  get glimpses of their life (real or imagined) and development of their character as the story unfolds. One of the main characters is Kif's best friend and Ziggy's bodygaurd, Ray, who is a colourful character and whose adventures with Kif recurs at reguar intervals. Kif's wife Sully, who is pregnant with twins and takes care of their daughter Bo when Kif is at his Office in Melbourne, is another interesting character whose slightest hint of a care directed at Kif regarding his writing progress leads them to embroil in bitter fights. And so the story goes.  Despite the sparse description of Ziggy --- which is by design --- he leaves a strong impression on your mind long after you finished reading the novel. I loved the philosophical ramblings and wise quips of the main characters especially Ziggy and Kif. For example, Zigggy often quotes from Tibbe ( a made up Phiolosopher, I assume) and his mental escapades becomes more and more meaningless (made mode deliberately so) as the books nears the end. And Kif's ruminations in the initial pages made him more relatable in my eyes. I would say, on the verge of nitpicking,  the novel is not without shortcomings abeit very few: Some events were predictable (maybe the predictability was not the point) and the ending seemed sudden and felt a little forced (Maybe I wanted to spnd some more time in this world). I have decided to enter into other worlds and words of this author from reading the first few pages of this novel, a rarity in my experience so far.
